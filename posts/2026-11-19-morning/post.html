AI agent security is now an operational risk for Indian companies, not a future one. According to a ServiceNow study released on 18 November 2026, 92% of Indian organisations had an AI agent security incident in the past year, and only 36% of them contained it within 24 hours. Most firms deployed agents faster than they built the controls to govern them.

This matters this week because agents are moving from chat windows into ERP, ticketing, plant and logistics systems, where they act rather than just answer. India's breach-reporting clock is short: CERT-In expects specified cyber incidents to be reported within six hours, and the DPDP Rules 2025 attach penalties of up to ₹250 crore to weak security safeguards. An agent nobody owns is a reporting problem waiting to happen.

What did the ServiceNow AI agent security study find?

It found that Indian firms have deployed AI agents widely but govern them weakly: 92% reported an agent-related security incident in the past year. The study, titled "Deployed but Ungoverned: The State of Agentic Security in India", surveyed 500 Indian security leaders as part of a global sample of 3,700 senior decision makers across 20 countries.

The gap is between confidence and controls. Half of Indian security leaders describe their agentic security as established, yet the same survey shows only 30% enforce an automated deployment gate that blocks agents until they pass security checks. Only 47% can produce a complete evidence package on demand, which is what an auditor or regulator will ask for after an incident. UNI's report of the study confirms the 92 per cent headline and the 19% accountability figure.

Why are AI agents harder to secure than ordinary software?

Because agents take actions with real credentials, and many are created outside IT's view. ServiceNow found 37% of Indian organisations discovered agents running in production that were never registered with security.

A traditional application does what its code says. An agent decides which tool to call, which record to update and which API to hit, based on prompts and data it reads at run time. That makes three things hard: knowing which agents exist, knowing what each one is allowed to touch, and stopping one quickly when it misbehaves. The survey shows 51% have automated real-time detection of anomalous agent behaviour, but 67% cannot automatically act when an agent goes wrong. Detection without a kill switch means a human still has to wake up and intervene.

The exposure is not limited to office software. ServiceNow reports that 83% of Indian respondents run agents in or interacting with operational technology or physical environments, yet only 29% have a separate governance framework designed for agents. For manufacturers, warehouses and hospitals, that means agents sit near machines, inventory and patient workflows.

What does an AI agent security incident cost an Indian company?

There is no agent-specific figure yet, but IBM puts India's average breach cost at ₹220 million in 2025, and shadow AI adds ₹17.9 million on top.

IBM's India data shows the average cost rose 13% from ₹195 million in 2024. Costs vary by sector: research was costliest at ₹289 million, followed by transportation at ₹288 million and industrial firms at ₹264 million. Only 42% of Indian organisations had policies to manage AI or detect shadow AI, which is exactly the blind spot that unregistered agents create.

The entry points also overlap with how agents work. IBM reports phishing at 18% and third-party vendor and supply chain compromise at 17% of initial attack vectors in India. Agents read emails, call vendor APIs and pass data between systems, so both vectors can now reach an agent with write access.

Does a unified agent security platform actually help?

Yes, on the numbers ServiceNow published: Indian firms running agentic security on a unified platform detect, fix and document incidents more often than those that do not.

About 43% of respondents run agentic security on one platform. Of those, 62% have automated real-time alerts against 43% of the rest, and 45% automatically remediate agent policy violations against 24%. Continuous evidence capture is 51% versus 43%. These are vendor-survey figures from a company that sells such a platform, so read them as direction, not proof. The direction is still sensible: one inventory, one policy layer and one log beat five tools that each see part of the picture.

What do CERT-In and the DPDP Rules require after an agent incident?

CERT-In requires specified cyber incidents to be reported within six hours of becoming aware, and the DPDP Rules require personal data breaches to be notified to the Data Protection Board.

The penalty schedule under the DPDP Rules 2025, notified on 14 November 2025, is steep: up to ₹250 crore for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards, ₹200 crore for failing to notify the Board or affected individuals of a breach, and ₹50 crore for other violations. The Rules give an eighteen-month phased compliance window, so most obligations bind from mid-2027. Volume is not small either: CERT-In handled over 29.44 lakh cyber incidents in 2025. An agent that leaks customer records through a tool call is a DPDP breach like any other, and it must be found and reported on the same clock.

How should Indian firms secure AI agents in the next 90 days?

Start with an inventory and an owner, then add a deployment gate and a kill switch. These five steps follow directly from the gaps in the data:

  1. Find every agent. Scan cloud accounts, low-code platforms, SaaS admin consoles and API keys for agents and bots. With 37% of firms finding unregistered agents, assume you have some.
  2. Name one owner. Give one person accountability for agentic AI governance, with authority over security, IT and business teams. Only 19% have done this.
  3. Gate deployment. No agent reaches production without a registered identity, least-privilege credentials, a defined tool list and a security review. Only 30% enforce this automatically today.
  4. Build the kill switch. Wire anomaly alerts to automatic actions: revoke tokens, pause the agent, roll back its last writes. This closes the 67% gap between detecting and acting.
  5. Log for the regulator. Keep a tamper-evident record of every agent action, prompt and data access, so an evidence package and a six-hour CERT-In report are possible.

Treat agents in OT and physical environments as a separate risk class with their own approval rules, given that 83% of firms already run them there.

What this means for Indian businesses

Agentic AI is not slowing down. Gartner, as reported by Outlook Business, expects 33% of enterprise software applications to include agentic AI by 2028, and at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions to be made autonomously. Gartner also predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, partly because of inadequate risk controls. The firms that keep their agents in production will be the ones that can show what each agent did and why.

For a mid-market manufacturer, distributor or hospital, the practical move is to design AI agent security into the build, not bolt it on after an incident. From the way we build custom AI systems, that means scoped credentials per agent, an approval step for high-impact actions and logs a compliance team can read. IBM's data also shows 73% of Indian organisations report limited or no use of AI and security automation, so there is room to improve on both sides. Frequently asked questions

What is AI agent security?

AI agent security is the set of controls that govern software agents which use AI to take actions, such as calling APIs, updating records or triggering workflows. It covers inventory, identity and permissions, deployment checks, real-time monitoring, automatic response and audit logs. ServiceNow's 2026 India study found 92% of Indian organisations had an agent-related security incident in the past year.

What is shadow AI in the context of AI agents?

Shadow AI refers to AI tools or agents running without the knowledge or approval of IT and security teams. ServiceNow found 37% of Indian organisations discovered unregistered agents running in production in the last 12 months. IBM's 2025 report found shadow AI added ₹17.9 million to the average cost of a data breach in India.

How fast must Indian companies report a cyber incident?

Under CERT-In's 2022 directions, specified cyber incidents must be reported within six hours of an organisation becoming aware of them. Personal data breaches must also be notified to the Data Protection Board under the DPDP Rules 2025, where failing to notify can attract a penalty of up to ₹200 crore. Agent incidents follow the same rules.

Who should own agentic AI governance in a company?

One named individual, usually the CISO or a senior technology leader, should be accountable across security, IT and business teams. ServiceNow found only 19% of Indian organisations have a single accountable owner today. Without one, agents built by business units fall between teams, which is how unregistered agents reach production.

Are AI agents in factories and hospitals riskier?

Yes, because agents that interact with machines, inventory or patient workflows can cause physical or safety consequences, not only data loss. ServiceNow found 83% of Indian organisations run agents in or around operational technology or physical environments, but only 29% have a separate governance framework designed for AI agents.
