LLM inference cost per token is collapsing, but total AI bills are not. Stanford HAI found the price of GPT-3.5-level output fell from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens between November 2022 and October 2024. Over the same period, usage exploded: agents now generate most tokens. For Indian firms, the budgeting question has shifted from price per token to tokens per task.

This matters now because 2026 is the year AI moved from chat windows into workflows. Menlo Ventures reports that US enterprise AI spending reached $176.8 billion by September 2026, up 5.4x in a year, even as unit prices kept falling. Indian CFOs signing off on AI pilots this quarter are seeing the same pattern on a smaller scale: the token rate looks trivial, the monthly invoice does not. This guide sets out token pricing benchmarks, the reasons AI agent costs rise, and a practical way to budget.

What is LLM inference cost?

LLM inference cost is what you pay each time a trained model reads input and writes output, usually billed per million tokens. Training builds the model once; inference is the running cost that grows with every user, document and agent step.

A token is a chunk of text. Gartner's analysis treats a token as roughly 4 characters, and a16z notes that one English word is about 1–2 tokens. Providers usually price input and output tokens separately, and output is typically the more expensive side. For an operations team, inference cost shows up in three places: API invoices from model providers, GPU hours if you host an open model yourself, and the engineering time spent on caching, routing and monitoring.

Inference is now where the money goes. Menlo Ventures found that for every dollar enterprises spent on model training in 2026, they spent $1.70 putting AI to work. Business Standard reports that training still accounts for around 80% of GPU hours on India's subsidised compute pool, but demand is shifting towards inference.

How fast are token prices falling?

Very fast: a16z estimates that for equivalent performance, LLM inference cost falls about 10x every year, roughly 1,000-fold over three years.

The data points line up across sources. When GPT-3 became publicly available in November 2021, it cost $60 per million tokens, according to a16z. By late 2024, the cheapest model reaching the same benchmark score, Llama 3.2 3B served on Together.ai, cost $0.06 per million tokens. Stanford HAI's AI Index 2025 shows a parallel drop at a higher capability level: GPT-3.5-equivalent performance went from $20 per million tokens to $0.07 per million tokens. Stanford also reports that, depending on the task, LLM inference prices have fallen anywhere from 9 to 900 times per year.

The forecast is more of the same. Gartner predicts that by 2030, running inference on a 1 trillion parameter LLM will cost GenAI providers over 90% less than in 2025, and that 2030 models will be up to 100 times more cost-efficient than the earliest models of similar size from 2022. Note that this is the provider's cost, not necessarily the price you pay.

Why are enterprise AI bills rising if tokens are cheaper?

Bills rise because volume grows faster than unit prices fall, and agents are the main reason volume is growing.

Menlo Ventures' November 2026 report shows enterprise AI spending jumped from $33 billion in 2025 to $176.8 billion by September 2026, accelerating from 3.9x growth the year before. Application spending alone went from $19 billion to $77 billion. The share of enterprises paying for AI rose only from 55% to 65%, so most of the growth came from existing buyers using far more, not from new buyers.

The token mix explains it. According to Menlo, agents now generate 80% of all tokens, and agent token consumption grew 37x while human-driven usage grew 5x. Gartner gives the per-task view: agentic models need between 5 and 30 times more tokens per task than a standard GenAI chatbot. An agent that plans, calls tools, reads results, retries and summarises runs the model many times for one business outcome. Each call is cheap. The chain is not.

Budgets also slip. Menlo's survey found enterprises budgeted nearly twice what they spent in 2025 and then spent about 2.5 times what they had budgeted. New budgets funded 55% of the incremental spend, and 22% came out of existing software budgets.

Where is the money going in 2026?

Most of it goes to coding tools, AI assistants and the three largest model labs, according to Menlo Ventures.

Coding accounted for $34 billion and AI assistants and productivity tools for $25 billion, together 76% of application spending. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google captured 69% of deployment spending, up from 50% in 2025. Healthcare remains the largest vertical market at $3.7 billion. Open-source models handle roughly one in five tokens but take only about one in 25 dollars of model spending, which shows the price gap between frontier and open models in practice.

Is self-hosting cheaper than API pricing in India?

Sometimes: for steady, high-volume workloads on a small open model, subsidised Indian GPUs can beat per-token API pricing; for spiky or frontier-quality workloads, APIs usually win.

India has unusually cheap compute on paper. When the IndiaAI compute portal launched in 2025, GoodReturns reported GPUs from ₹67 per hour, and the IndiaAI Mission was approved with a budget of ₹10,371.92 crore, with nearly 45% of funds earmarked for compute. By mid-2026, Business Standard reported access at around ₹65 per GPU hour under a programme backed by more than ₹10,000 crore, with more than 38,000 GPUs available across the broader ecosystem. More than 60% of startups requesting access want GPUs for inference.

The catch is utilisation. A rented GPU costs the same whether it serves one request or ten thousand. Self-hosting pays off when you can keep GPUs busy, when data residency rules out foreign APIs, or when a small fine-tuned model is good enough for a narrow task. Business Standard also notes practical limits, such as a seven-day lease cap that complicates longer runs. For most mid-market firms, the sensible pattern is API first, then move the highest-volume, most predictable workload to an open model once you have six to eight weeks of real usage data.

How should Indian firms budget for LLM inference cost?

Budget per completed task, not per token: estimate tokens per task, multiply by task volume and price, then add headroom for agent loops and growth.

  1. Define the unit of work. One invoice matched, one claim summarised, one purchase order checked. Cost per unit is the number your CFO will understand.
  2. Measure tokens per task in a pilot. Log input and output tokens for every call. Agent flows can use 5 to 30 times more tokens per task than a single chatbot answer, per Gartner, so measure rather than guess.
  3. Route by difficulty. Send simple classification and extraction to small, cheap models; reserve frontier models for reasoning-heavy steps. The 10x yearly price fall applies mostly to yesterday's capability level.
  4. Cache and trim. Reuse system prompts, cache repeated context, cap retries and limit how many documents an agent can pull per step.
  5. Set hard limits. Per-user and per-workflow spend caps, with alerts well before a cap is hit.
  6. Review quarterly. Re-run the same test set on newer, cheaper models every quarter. Prices move faster than annual budgets.

What this means for Indian businesses

For Indian manufacturers, distributors, hospitals and lenders, the falling LLM inference cost is real, but it is not a reason to skip cost design. Stanford HAI reports that 78% of organisations surveyed used AI in 2024, up from 55% in 2023, and generative AI use in at least one function rose from 33% to 71%. Adoption is now the default. The firms that keep margins are the ones that treat inference as an operating cost with a unit price, an owner and a monthly review, the same way they treat freight or power.

Frequently asked questions

How much does LLM inference cost per million tokens in 2026?

It depends heavily on the model tier. Small and mid-sized models now cost cents per million tokens: Stanford HAI recorded $0.07 for GPT-3.5-level performance by October 2024. Frontier reasoning models cost far more, and a16z noted OpenAI's o1 launched at the same output price GPT-3 had at launch. Always compare price at the capability level your task needs.

Why do AI agents cost more than chatbots?

Agents call the model many times per task: to plan, use tools, read results, check work and retry. Gartner estimates agentic models need between 5 and 30 times more tokens per task than a standard GenAI chatbot. Menlo Ventures reports that agents already generate 80% of enterprise tokens, which is why total bills rise even as token prices fall.

Will LLM inference keep getting cheaper?

Most evidence says yes. a16z measured roughly a 10x yearly decline for equivalent performance, and Gartner forecasts that inference on a 1 trillion parameter model will cost providers over 90% less in 2030 than in 2025. Gartner's figure is the provider's cost, so plan for cheaper units but not proportionally cheaper invoices.

Can Indian companies use IndiaAI GPUs for inference?

Yes, IndiaAI GPU capacity serves inference as well as training. Business Standard reports subsidised access at around ₹65 per GPU hour, and more than 60% of startups requesting access want GPUs for inference. Eligibility, voucher terms and lease limits apply, so check the IndiaAI compute portal for current rules before planning a production workload around it.

Is open-source cheaper than proprietary models?

Per token, usually. Menlo Ventures found open-source models handle about one in five enterprise tokens but take only about one in 25 dollars of model spend. The trade-off is engineering effort: hosting, scaling, monitoring and security become your job, and quality on complex reasoning may lag frontier models. Many firms use both, routing routine steps to open models.
