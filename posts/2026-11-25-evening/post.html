How much customer service work does AI handle in India?

Between roughly a third and just over half of Indian service volume now runs through AI, depending on how you count. Twilio reports AI agents handle 55% of all customer service interactions in India, and business leaders expect 74% by 2027.

Salesforce's State of Service research, which surveyed 300 Indian service professionals, gives a more conservative read: teams estimate AI handles 30% of cases today and expect 50% by 2027. The gap is mostly definitional. "Interactions" includes every chat turn and status check; "cases" are tickets that need resolution. Either way, the direction is the same, and the 2027 targets are not far off.

Adoption is also broadening in format. Salesforce found 44% of Indian service teams now use multimodal AI, handling voice, images and text rather than chat alone. On the customer side, ServiceNow found 80% of Indian consumers already use AI chatbots for status checks, product recommendations and self-help guides.

Why do Indian customers abandon AI agents?

They abandon AI agents mostly because the agent does not know who they are. Twilio found 68% of Indian consumers stopped using an AI agent mid-interaction because it lacked basic information about them, and 5% walked away from the brand entirely.

The repetition problem shows up everywhere in the data. According to Twilio, 75% of Indian consumers say they repeat information to an AI agent, and 51% repeat themselves again after the bot hands them to a human. Brands do not see it: 89% of Indian brands believe their AI agents recognise returning customers. That is a gap between what the dashboard says and what the customer experiences.

This is a data plumbing problem more than a model problem. A bot that cannot read the order history, the open ticket, or the last call note will ask the customer for it. The model can be excellent and still fail on this.

Do customers actually prefer AI chatbots?

Increasingly yes, for routine tasks, as long as a human is reachable. Twilio found 67% of Indian consumers are more likely to choose an AI chatbot over a human than a year ago, and 83% say bots improved over the past year.

Speed is the main draw. Twilio reports AI agents save Indian consumers an average of 101 seconds per interaction. ServiceNow found 82% of Indian customers say new AI tools have raised their expectations of service, so faster bots also reset the bar for everyone else on the team.

The condition is control. Gartner's survey of 3,566 customers found 50% say interactions are easier when companies use GenAI, but 87% say an option to reach a human agent is essential. Customers are also using their own tools: Gartner reports customers were about three times more likely to use third-party GenAI such as ChatGPT or Gemini than a company's own chatbot in their most recent service interaction. If your bot is worse than a general assistant, customers will go around it.

What do Indian customers expect from AI service agents?

They expect honesty about the bot, a fast exit to a human, and visibility into their data. Brands are behind on all three, according to Twilio's 2026 India data.

Twilio found 71% of Indian consumers want brands to declare AI at the start of a conversation. Most brands (86%) say their bots identify as AI at some point, but only 31% do so from the first message. On escalation, 59% want an immediate option to switch to a human, while 49% of businesses say they make that easier. Another 59% want clear visibility into the personal data the AI can access, and only 37% of businesses let customers control whether their data trains AI models.

For high-stakes actions, consent matters most. Twilio reports 57% of Indian consumers say requiring human approval for high-stakes AI actions is essential to their confidence. Refunds, cancellations and account changes should have a checkpoint.

Is AI replacing customer service agents?

Not at scale so far. Gartner's survey of 275 service leaders, fielded in July and August 2026, found only 21% have reduced human agent headcount because of AI.

Among those that did cut, the average reduction was 16.7% of the workforce over 18 months. Some are reversing course: Gartner reports 7% have already rehired after reductions and 11% plan to rehire within 18 months. The more common moves are softer. Gartner found 71% of leaders are shifting agents into different roles, 45% report a hiring freeze, 39% are moving agents into other business units, and only 20% use forced reductions.

Indian agents are more optimistic than the headlines. ServiceNow found 52% of Indian customer service agents believe AI will enhance their role and 48% see it as a way to reduce workload. Gartner's earlier survey found 58% of service leaders plan to upskill agents into knowledge management specialists, which is the job that keeps an AI agent accurate.

What is the real cost of poor customer service in India?

The cost shows up as churn and public complaints. ServiceNow found 89% of Indian consumers are willing to switch brands over slow or inefficient service, and 84% would post a negative review after a bad experience.

The friction points are familiar. According to ServiceNow, 39% of Indian consumers are still stuck waiting on hold or in a queue, 36% are frustrated by being transferred between people or departments, and 34% believe organisations make it intentionally hard to register complaints. Those are exactly the problems an AI front line with good context can remove, and exactly the ones a poorly connected bot makes worse.

How to roll out AI customer service: a 6-step playbook

From the way we build service systems, the order of work matters more than the choice of model. A practical sequence:

  1. Pick three to five high-volume, low-risk intents. Order status, appointment changes, document requests and FAQs. Measure their current volume and handle time first.
  2. Connect the customer record before launch. The bot should see identity, order or account history and open tickets. This addresses the memory failure behind the 68% abandonment figure.
  3. Disclose AI in the first message. It costs nothing and closes the 71% vs 31% gap in one line of copy.
  4. Design the human handoff. One tap to a person, with the full transcript passed across so the customer never repeats themselves.
  5. Gate high-stakes actions. Refunds, cancellations and data changes need human approval or a confirmation step.
  6. Retrain agents as AI supervisors. Assign owners for the knowledge base, review failed conversations weekly, and track containment, resolution and repeat-contact rates together.

What this means for Indian businesses

Indian customers have already moved: they use chatbots daily and expect speed. The differentiator is no longer whether you have an AI agent but whether it knows the customer and hands off cleanly. For hospitals, distributors, NBFCs and retailers, that usually means integrating the bot with the ERP, HMS or CRM rather than buying a standalone widget. Start narrow, connect the data, and expand intent by intent.

Frequently asked questions

What share of customer service is handled by AI in India?

Twilio's 2026 research says AI agents handle 55% of customer service interactions in India, with business leaders expecting 74% by 2027. Salesforce's survey of Indian service teams, which counts resolved cases rather than interactions, puts it at 30% today and 50% by 2027. Both point to AI handling at least half of service volume within two years.

Why do customers drop AI chatbots?

The most common reason is missing context. Twilio found 68% of Indian consumers abandoned an AI agent mid-conversation because it lacked basic information about them, and 75% say they repeat information to bots. Connecting the bot to order, account and ticket history fixes most of this before any model tuning is needed.

Should an AI customer service bot say it is AI?

Yes. Twilio found 71% of Indian consumers want brands to declare AI at the start of a conversation, while only 31% of brands do so from the first message. Disclosure at the start is simple to implement and builds the trust needed for customers to accept AI handling routine requests.

Will AI replace customer service agents?

Current data says mostly no. Gartner found only 21% of service leaders have reduced agent headcount because of AI, while 71% are moving agents into new roles. Some firms that cut are rehiring: 7% already have and 11% plan to. The likely result is smaller, more skilled teams that supervise AI and handle complex cases.

Do customers still want a human option?

Yes. Gartner's survey of 3,566 customers found 87% say companies using GenAI for service must offer access to a human agent. In India, Twilio found 59% want an immediate option to switch to a person. A clear handoff with the transcript passed across is a basic requirement, not an extra.
