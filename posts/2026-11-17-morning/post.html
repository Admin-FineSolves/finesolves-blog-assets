WPI inflation in India rose to 12.29% in October 2026, up from 11.39% in September, according to provisional Ministry of Commerce and Industry data released on 16 November. Fuel and power drove the jump, with that group's wholesale prices up 32.84% year on year. For manufacturers, distributors and hospitals, this is an input-cost shock that will reach purchase orders and supplier contracts within weeks.

Wholesale prices are what businesses pay each other before goods reach a shop shelf. When the wholesale price index moves this fast, margins get squeezed first and consumer prices follow later. October's reading lands alongside retail inflation above the RBI's comfort band and a record trade gap, so this is not a one-line economics story. It is a procurement, pricing and working-capital problem for the next two quarters.

TL;DR

  • Headline WPI inflation reached 12.29% in October 2026, from 11.39% in September and 9.92% in August.
  • Fuel and power wholesale prices rose 32.84%; crude petroleum and natural gas rose 70.80%.
  • Industrial inputs are expensive: chemicals up 17.53%, basic metals up 17.21%, minerals up 12.64%.
  • Manufactured products eased slightly to 9.54%, from 9.76%, so factories are absorbing part of the cost.
  • Retail CPI inflation hit 6.09%, above the RBI's 6% upper tolerance limit.

What did the October 2026 WPI data show?

Wholesale inflation accelerated to 12.29% in October 2026, with fuel and power doing most of the work while manufactured goods cooled slightly. Business Standard reported that the provisional figure is based on a weighted response rate of 81.1%, so a revision is possible when the final number comes out.

The month-to-month path matters more than any single print. The final August figure was 9.92%, September came in at 11.39%, and October added almost another point. Over April to October 2026-27, cumulative WPI inflation stands at 10.14%, according to ETV Bharat's report of the ministry data, against a contraction of 0.30% in the same period a year earlier. That swing is what procurement budgets built last spring did not plan for.

Why is fuel and power inflation so high?

Fuel and power inflation is high because crude oil, natural gas and refined fuel prices are far above last year's levels, and electricity prices have turned from falling to rising. Goodreturns reported fuel and power wholesale inflation at 32.84% in October, up from 25.85% in September.

Inside that group, crude petroleum and natural gas rose 70.80%, from 57.32% a month earlier. Mineral oils, which include diesel, petrol and other refined products, rose 45.57%, from 40.02%, according to The Tribune's report of the data. Electricity flipped from a contraction of 0.43% in September to an increase of 8.06% in October. For an energy-heavy plant, that last number is the one to watch, because power tariffs reset more slowly than diesel and rarely come back down quickly.

The external context explains the pressure. India imports nearly 85% of its crude requirement, and Angel One noted that disruption in global energy markets after the Middle East conflict has added to import costs. Brent has since fallen to around $92 a barrel, which offers some relief, but wholesale data lags spot prices. Across April to October, fuel and power inflation has averaged 26.37%.

Which industrial inputs are getting more expensive?

Chemicals, basic metals, minerals and non-food raw materials are rising fastest outside energy, each well above the 9.54% rate for manufactured products overall. That gap is the margin squeeze in numbers.

ETV Bharat's report of the release shows chemicals and chemical products up 17.53% (from 16.74%) and basic metals up 17.21% (from 16.96%). The Federal reported minerals at 12.64% and non-food articles, which include fibres and oilseeds, at 11.69%. The ministry statement also named textiles and rubber and plastic products as contributors, without separate figures in the reports we read.

Now compare those input rates with output. Manufactured products inflation eased to 9.54%, and the April to October average is 8.37%. When chemicals and metals rise at 17.53% and 17.21% while finished goods rise at 9.54%, someone is eating the difference. In most supply chains that is the mid-sized manufacturer or distributor with the least pricing power.

How does wholesale inflation compare with retail inflation?

Wholesale inflation at 12.29% is roughly double retail inflation at 6.09%, which means more cost pressure is still in the pipeline for consumers. Goodreturns reported that October CPI breached the RBI's 6% upper tolerance limit.

Food is moving in opposite directions at the two levels. The WPI Food Index, which carries a weight of 24.99% in the wholesale basket, eased to 8.58%. The Consumer Food Price Index rose to 8.94%, from 7.74% in September. The Federal's report points to an expected RBI rate hike next month. For businesses, a hike would raise the cost of the working capital needed to carry dearer inventory.

Trade data from the same week adds another layer. Merchandise exports hit a record $49.47 billion in October, up 45.06% year on year, according to Angel One's report of commerce ministry data. Imports were $89.35 billion, and the merchandise trade deficit widened to $39.88 billion, the highest in a year. A large import bill driven by energy keeps pressure on the rupee, which feeds back into imported input costs.

What should procurement and operations teams do now?

Procurement teams should re-price contracts to an index, protect energy-heavy cost lines, and forecast input costs every week rather than every quarter. Here is a practical sequence for the next 30 days.

  1. Map your exposure. Split your top 50 purchased items by WPI group: fuel, chemicals, metals, minerals, food. Most firms find that a handful of SKUs carry most of the inflation risk.
  2. Index the contracts that matter. For long-running supply agreements, link price revisions to the relevant published WPI sub-index with a cap and floor, instead of renegotiating line by line every month.
  3. Separate energy from everything else. Track diesel, gas and power as their own cost centre. With mineral oils up 45.57%, freight and generator costs deserve a weekly review, not a monthly one.
  4. Forecast landed cost, not just demand. A forecasting model that ingests WPI releases, commodity prices and supplier quotes can flag margin erosion before invoices arrive. This is where AI in procurement earns its keep.
  5. Revisit working capital. Higher input costs plus a possible rate hike means inventory gets more expensive to hold. Recheck safety stock levels on slow movers.
  6. Pass through deliberately. Decide which customers and products can absorb a price revision, and time it to the data rather than to complaints from sales.

What this means for Indian businesses

For Indian SMB and mid-market firms, the October data says input costs are rising faster than selling prices, and that the gap is widest in energy, chemicals and metals. Logistics operators carry the fuel shock directly. Hospitals and clinics feel electricity, consumables and chemical inputs together. Manufacturers with fixed-price customer contracts are most exposed.

The firms that come through periods like this tend to have one thing in common: they see cost changes early. Frequently asked questions

What is WPI inflation in India in October 2026?

WPI inflation in India was 12.29% in October 2026, according to provisional data released by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry on 16 November 2026. It rose from 11.39% in September and 9.92% in August. Fuel and power prices, up 32.84% year on year, were the main driver of the increase.

What is the difference between WPI and CPI inflation?

WPI measures price changes in bulk transactions between businesses, such as raw materials, fuel and factory goods. CPI measures the prices households pay at retail. In October 2026 WPI inflation was 12.29% while CPI inflation was 6.09%, which suggests more cost pressure may still pass through to consumers in the coming months.

Why did wholesale inflation rise in October 2026?

Wholesale inflation rose mainly because of fuel and power. Crude petroleum and natural gas prices were up 70.80%, mineral oils 45.57% and electricity 8.06%. The ministry also cited food articles, basic metals, chemicals, textiles and rubber and plastic products. India imports nearly 85% of its crude, so global energy disruption feeds straight into domestic wholesale prices.

How does high WPI inflation affect manufacturers?

High WPI inflation raises the cost of raw materials, fuel and power faster than finished-goods prices. In October 2026, chemicals rose 17.53% and basic metals 17.21%, while manufactured products rose only 9.54%. That gap squeezes margins, especially for firms on fixed-price contracts, and increases the working capital needed to carry inventory.

Will the RBI raise interest rates because of inflation?

A rate hike is now widely expected. Retail CPI inflation reached 6.09% in October 2026, above the RBI's 6% upper tolerance limit, and The Federal's report points to an expected hike at the next policy meeting. Businesses should plan for higher borrowing costs on working capital and review inventory levels accordingly.
