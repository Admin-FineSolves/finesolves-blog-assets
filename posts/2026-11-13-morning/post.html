Indigenous AI models are moving from showcase to procurement question. On 12 November 2026, MediaNama, citing a Business Standard report, said the government is likely to push critical sectors to use indigenous AI models, after an official said some domestic models reach up to 80% of the capacity of leading frontier models. For Indian firms in BFSI, health and public supply chains, model choice may soon carry a compliance angle.

Why now: in the space of ten days, the IndiaAI Mission unveiled three new foundation models, Tracxn data showed Indian AI startup funding at $1.6 billion for January to September 2026, and reports on how the mission spends its compute subsidy became public. Read together, the numbers say India is building a sovereign AI stack fast, but unevenly. Here is what the data shows and what operators should do about it.

TL;DR

  • The IndiaAI Mission has approved 20 AI models, built by 12 startups, according to MediaNama's report.
  • Soket's EKA-1 has 26.3 billion parameters, trained on about 7.5 trillion tokens, covering 22 scheduled languages plus English.
  • Indian AI startups raised $1.6 billion in January–September 2026, up from $677 million a year earlier, per Tracxn.

  • Foundation-model builders received 83% of IndiaAI compute subsidy; researchers got 0.8%, ThePrint found.

What did the government signal about indigenous AI models?

The government is reportedly preparing to steer critical sectors towards indigenous AI models, though no formal rule has been published yet. MediaNama's 12 November report, based on Business Standard, describes a push rather than a mandate.

The case rests on capability claims. An official quoted in the report said some domestic models now reach up to 80% of the capacity of leading frontier models. The IndiaAI Mission has approved the development of 20 AI models, a mix of general-purpose large language models and sector-specific small language models, being built by 12 startups selected under the mission. Gnani, Gan AI, Fractal, Tech Mahindra and Shodh AI are due to release models over the next 12–15 months, the report says.

"Critical sectors" is not yet defined in public. Based on how India treats data localisation elsewhere, banks, insurers, hospitals, telecom and government departments are the obvious candidates. Treat this as a direction of travel, not a deadline.

Which indigenous AI models launched this month?

Three foundation models launched under the IndiaAI Mission in early November: Soket's EKA-1 and ZenteiQ's Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B. All three are released with open weights.

According to News On AIR, EKA-1 is an open, sparse hybrid model with 26.3 billion parameters, trained on approximately 7.5 trillion tokens, and it spans all 22 scheduled Indian languages alongside English. It can be deployed in air-gapped environments, which matters for regulated buyers. Swarajya reported that the Dhīr models support English and 10 Indian languages, can run on mobile phones without internet, and were trained from scratch on Google Cloud TPUs. The 3 models' weights are published under the Apache 2.0 licence through AIKosh.

Where is AI startup funding in India going?

AI startup funding in India is going mostly into infrastructure, which took 75% of the $1.6 billion raised in January–September 2026. That is according to Tracxn data reported by Elets CIO on 12 November.

The headline number more than doubled from $677 million a year earlier, but the base narrowed. Rounds fell 43%, and four deals made up 72% of the total: Neysa raised $600 million in a round led by Blackstone, Sarvam $255 million, Yotta $150 million and Emergent $130 million. AI infrastructure alone raised $1.2 billion across 19 rounds, 8.3 times the $145 million raised a year earlier.

Applications went the other way. AI application startups raised $390 million, down 27% from $532 million. Vertical AI fell 87% to $41 million from $319 million, while enterprise AI apps rose 70% to $348 million. The same report notes GPUs in Indian data centres run at about 20% utilisation, citing Rhodium Group research.

Who gets the IndiaAI compute subsidy?

Foundation-model builders get almost all of it: 83% of the compute subsidy went to 16 model-builder approvals, ThePrint found in its analysis of 304 approvals on the IndiaAI Compute Portal.

Nine foundation-model organisations received ₹1,058 crore of the ₹1,275 crore committed so far. Researchers, students and fellows received ₹10.14 crore, or 0.8%. Government departments and PSUs took 12.5% across 123 approvals. Most users get 40% of their bill paid, but the report shows model builders had 98.9% of their bills covered, against 46.8% for government bodies and 39.7% for researchers.

The subsidised rate is cheap by global standards. Communications Today, citing PIB, puts it at ₹100 an hour against a global market rate of $2.5 to 3 an hour, and ThePrint cites a tender rate of about ₹140 an hour for an H100. An earlier PIB factsheet listed 34,381 GPUs on the portal, and a Lok Sabha reply in August reported 93.18 lakh GPU hours sanctioned across 237 projects.

Is the IndiaAI Mission spending its budget?

Not at the planned pace. Releases have trailed estimates every year, according to a MeitY reply in the Rajya Sabha reported by MediaNama.

The mission has a five-year outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore. In 2024–25, ₹21.79 crore was released against a revised estimate of ₹173 crore. In 2025–26, ₹379.15 crore was released against ₹800 crore. As of April 2026, nothing had been released against the 2026–27 budget estimate of ₹1,000 crore.

Slow releases are common for new missions, and the figures predate the latest model launches. Still, they matter for buyers: the domestic model ecosystem is young, and its timelines depend partly on public money arriving on time.

Should Indian businesses switch to indigenous AI models now?

Not wholesale. Design so you can switch, and pilot an indigenous model where language coverage, data residency or air-gapped deployment already matter.

Swapping out a working stack because of a reported policy direction is poor value. Locking yourself into one foreign API is also a risk if critical-sector guidance arrives. The sensible middle path is a model-agnostic architecture plus a measured pilot.

  1. Map your exposure. List every workflow that sends customer, patient or financial data to a model. Flag which ones sit in sectors likely to be called critical.
  2. Put a gateway in front of models. Route all calls through one internal layer with logging, so changing models is a config change, not a rewrite.
  3. Build an evaluation set from your own data. Use 100–200 real, anonymised tasks in the languages your customers use. Public benchmarks will not tell you how a model handles your Gujarati invoices or Hindi claim notes.
  4. Pilot an open-weight Indian model on one narrow task. Document summarisation, regional-language support or form extraction are good first candidates. Compare accuracy, latency and cost against your current model.
  5. Check deployment options. Open weights under Apache 2.0 allow on-premise or private-cloud hosting. Price the GPUs, including the subsidised IndiaAI rate if you qualify.
  6. Write the exit plan. Record which prompts, guardrails and integrations would need to change. Keep it current.

What this means for Indian businesses

Three practical points for founders, CXOs and IT heads. First, if you sell to banks, hospitals or government, expect model provenance questions in RFPs before any formal rule. Having a pilot with an indigenous model gives you an answer. Second, the funding data says capital is flowing to compute, not to sector-specific applications. That leaves room for firms that build practical, domain-specific tools on top of open models. Third, cheap subsidised compute only helps if your team can use it; the 20% utilisation figure is a reminder that GPUs without workloads are a cost.

No. As of 13 November 2026, there is no published mandate. MediaNama, citing Business Standard, reported that the government is likely to push critical sectors to use indigenous AI models. Firms in banking, insurance, healthcare and government supply should prepare for guidance, but nothing requires them to switch models today.

What is EKA-1?

EKA-1 is a foundation model built by Soket under the IndiaAI Mission. According to News On AIR, it has 26.3 billion parameters, was trained on approximately 7.5 trillion tokens, and covers all 22 scheduled Indian languages plus English. It can run in air-gapped environments, and its weights are available through AIKosh.

How good are Indian foundation models compared with global ones?

An official quoted in MediaNama's report said some domestic models reach up to 80% of the capacity of leading frontier models. That is a self-reported comparison, not an independent benchmark. Test any model on your own tasks and languages before deciding, because performance varies widely by use case.

Can a private company use IndiaAI subsidised GPUs?

Yes, startups, MSMEs, researchers and government bodies can apply through the IndiaAI Compute Portal. Most users get 40% of the bill paid, per ThePrint. Communications Today, citing PIB, puts the subsidised rate at ₹100 an hour. Expect documentation requirements and approval time before compute is available.

How much did Indian AI startups raise in 2026?

Indian AI startups raised $1.6 billion across 81 rounds in January–September 2026, up from $677 million a year earlier, according to Tracxn data reported by Elets CIO. Four deals made up 72% of the total, and AI infrastructure companies took 75% of all funding.
