Legacy system modernization is the work of moving old, hard-to-change business software onto architecture you can maintain, integrate and extend. It pays when it is scoped tightly, and it overruns when it is not: Ensono's 2026 survey found 71% of organizations exceeded their original modernization budgets, while Kyndryl reports 288% to 362% ROI on mainframe programmes that land.

This matters in India right now because the money is already moving. According to Bain's India Enterprise Technology Report 2026, Indian IT spending is likely to rise about 6% to 8% this year, and capex makes up 50% to 60% of Indian IT budgets, against 20% to 30% for global peers. A large slice of that capex is going into replacing or rebuilding legacy software. The question for operators is how to spend it without joining the overrun statistics.

TL;DR

  • 71% of organizations went over their original modernization budget, and 61% paused, delayed, scaled back or abandoned a programme in the last 24 months (Ensono, 2026).
  • Core application modernization takes 25% of Indian IT capex, and data modernization and AI another 30% (Bain, 2026).
  • About 70% of Indian leaders say the technical debt in legacy systems will take significant time to overcome (Bain).

  • Mainframe modernization projects report 288% to 362% ROI when they land (Kyndryl, 2025).

What is legacy system modernization, in plain terms?

It is replacing, rebuilding or re-platforming business software that has become expensive to run and risky to change, so it can integrate with new tools, data and AI.

In Indian mid-market and enterprise firms, "legacy" rarely means a mainframe. More often it is a 12-year-old desktop ERP customised beyond recognition, an Access or FoxPro billing tool one person understands, a homegrown dispatch system with no API, or a stack of Excel macros holding up month-end. Bain's report notes that systems built in earlier spending waves lost their edge and became legacy within 3–5 years. That is the real clock: software ages faster than the asset register suggests.

Modernization is not one thing. It ranges from moving an application to new infrastructure unchanged, to rewriting it as services, to retiring it for a product. Picking the wrong level of change for a given system is the single most common way budgets slip.

How big is the legacy problem for Indian businesses?

Large: about 90% of Indian leaders in Bain's survey say their data foundations are weak and not fit to scale. About 70% say legacy technical debt will take significant time to overcome.

Bain surveyed more than 250 CIOs, CDOs and CXOs in India for the 2026 report. Only 15% of business leaders said they see IT as truly strategic, and another 70% called it good but not great. Three in four cited a gap between business-unit and IT goals. Those numbers describe the operating reality behind legacy estates: systems built for one era's processes, maintained by teams who are measured on uptime rather than change.

The spend is following. Bain puts data modernization and AI at 30% of Indian IT capex, core application modernization at 25%, cloud and IT infrastructure at 25% and cybersecurity at 20%.

Together, data modernization and AI plus core application modernization make up more than half of Indian IT capex. Bain estimates that firms which get this right could see a 15% to 20% absolute EBITDA improvement. That is the prize. The overrun data below is the risk.

Why do so many modernization projects go over budget?

Because the legacy system is usually bigger, more connected and less documented than the plan assumed, and teams try to modernize everything at once while still running the old estate.

Ensono's 2026 State of IT Modernization report found 71% of organizations exceeded their original modernization budgets. Over the past 24 months, 38% paused or delayed initiatives, 36% scaled them back and 16% abandoned them outright, for a combined 61% that hit a serious stall. The year before, Ensono's 2025 report found 49% saw actual legacy maintenance costs exceed planned costs, and budget crunches burdened innovation for 86% of respondents.

A caveat on the sample: Ensono's research covers US and UK organizations, and its 2025 edition surveyed 500 IT decision-makers at firms with at least $500 million in annual revenue. Indian mid-market firms run smaller estates, but the failure modes rhyme. From the way we build, the overruns we see in India come from the same three places: undocumented business rules hiding in old code, integrations nobody listed, and data that needs cleaning before it can move.

Is legacy modernization worth it? The ROI and budget data

Yes, when it is staged: Kyndryl's 2025 survey of 500 business and IT leaders reports 288% to 362% ROI on mainframe modernization, and McKinsey finds deliberate modernizers spend far more of their budget on change.

Kyndryl's respondents collectively reported $13 billion in savings and forecast $20 billion in new revenues over the next three years. The survey also found 80% had shifted their modernization strategy in the past year, a sign that the plan written at kick-off rarely survives contact with the old system.

The budget-mix data is more useful for Indian CFOs. McKinsey's 2026 analysis of 17 global companies groups IT organizations into four archetypes by how they split spending between run (keeping systems going) and change (building new capability).

It is that every rupee you stop spending on keeping an old system alive is a rupee available for change. Modernization is how you move money from the first column to the second.

Which legacy modernization approach fits which system?

Match the approach to the system's business value and how often it must change: rehost stable systems, refactor valuable ones, replace commodity ones with products, and retire anything nobody needs.

Regulation shapes this choice more than many teams expect. Kyndryl found 94% of respondents said regulatory compliance strongly influences their modernization plans. For Indian BFSI, healthcare and any firm handling personal data under the DPDP Act, data residency, audit trails and retention rules decide what can be rehosted, what must be rebuilt and what can be retired.

How do you run a legacy modernization programme without overrunning?

Run it as a series of small, funded releases against measured outcomes, not as one big-bang replacement with a fixed end date.

  1. Inventory before you estimate. List every system, its users, integrations, data stores and the person who understands it. Budgets slip on what was never listed.
  2. Score each system on value and pain. Business criticality, change frequency, run cost, security exposure and key-person risk. This tells you which approach from the table to use.
  3. Extract the business rules first. Old code holds pricing logic, tax handling and approval rules nobody wrote down. Document them, and test the new system against them.
  4. Fix the data in parallel. With about 90% of Indian leaders calling their data foundations weak, migration is where timelines go to die. Clean, de-duplicate and map master data early.
  5. Ship one module in 6–10 weeks. Pick a contained, visible workflow, run old and new side by side, and switch over once numbers reconcile.
  6. Retire as you go. Each release should switch something off. Run cost only falls when old systems actually stop.
  7. Budget for skills. Ensono's 2025 report found 9 in 10 respondents had at least one talent gap, with cloud architecture and migration cited by 27%. Kyndryl found 74% rely on third-party providers, and Ensono found 95% turn to partners.

Does AI make legacy modernization faster?

Increasingly, yes: 54% of decision-makers in Ensono's 2026 report say AI has accelerated modernization, up from 39% in 2025, mostly by speeding up code analysis, documentation and testing.

The gains are real but bounded. The same Ensono research found 33% struggle to integrate AI into existing workflows and 28% cite infrastructure limitations as a top barrier to AI ROI. AI is good at reading old code and drafting documentation and tests; it does not decide which business rules still matter. The share of IT team time spent innovating rose to 38% from 25% in 2025, but 47% of respondents say they remain focused primarily on day-to-day operations, and 93% say innovation is frequently delayed.

The useful framing: AI lowers the cost of understanding a legacy system, which used to be the most expensive and least visible part of modernization. It also raises the stakes, because most AI use cases need clean data and APIs that legacy systems do not provide.

What this means for Indian businesses

Indian firms are spending more on IT than global peers, and more of it is capex. That is a window. Ensono found 78% of decision-makers say legacy systems matter more to their organization than two years ago, and only 31% consider themselves modernization leaders, up from 21% in 2025. The firms that move from "keep it running" to staged, measured modernization will have the clean data and integration layer that AI needs; the rest will keep paying run costs on systems that age every year.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between legacy modernization and digital transformation?

Legacy modernization is the technical work of replacing, rebuilding or re-platforming old business software. Digital transformation is the broader change in processes, products and operating model. Modernization is usually a prerequisite: you cannot redesign a process around data and integrations that an old system cannot provide, so most transformation programmes stall without it.

How long does legacy system modernization take?

A single contained module can usually be rebuilt and switched over in weeks to a few months; a full estate takes years. The data shows big-bang timelines rarely hold: Ensono found 61% of organizations paused, delayed, scaled back or abandoned modernization initiatives in the past 24 months. Staged releases keep the timeline honest.

Should we rebuild a legacy system or replace it with SaaS?

Replace it with a product when the process is generic and you gain nothing from doing it differently, such as payroll or email. Rebuild when the system encodes how you compete, such as pricing, dispatch, scheduling or clinical workflows. Wrapping the old core with APIs is a reasonable interim step when budgets are tight.

What is the biggest risk in a legacy modernization project?

Underestimating what the old system actually does. Undocumented business rules, unlisted integrations and dirty data cause most overruns, which is why Ensono found 71% of organizations exceeded their original modernization budgets. Inventory every system and extract its business rules before committing to a fixed estimate.

How much of the IT budget should go to modernization?

There is no single right figure, but McKinsey's 2026 data is a useful benchmark: deliberate modernizers put 37% of IT budget into change and 63% into run, while lean operators put just 13% into change. Track your own run-versus-change split and aim to shift it each year as old systems are retired.
