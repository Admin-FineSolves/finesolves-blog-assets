India exports of goods and services reached an estimated $89.99 billion in October 2026, up 29.90% from a year earlier, according to Ministry of Commerce and Industry data released on 13 November. Merchandise exports hit a record $49.47 billion. Imports grew more slowly, so the combined trade deficit narrowed to $20.09 billion, from $25.15 billion in October 2025.

This is the strongest monthly export print India has recorded, and it arrives with a catch. The goods-only trade gap is still wide at $39.88 billion, well above the $32.25 billion a Reuters poll expected, as NiftyTrader reported. For exporters, manufacturers and the ops teams behind them, the October data says two things at once: overseas demand is there, and input costs, especially fuel, are rising just as fast. Here is what the numbers show and what to do with them.

TL;DR

  • Total exports were an estimated $89.99 billion in October 2026, up 29.90%; total imports were $110.07 billion, up 16.58%.
  • Merchandise exports rose 45.06% to a record $49.47 billion, from $34.11 billion a year earlier.
  • Electronic goods exports grew 62.70% to $6.64 billion; engineering goods grew 36.01% to $12.74 billion.
  • The merchandise trade deficit was $39.88 billion, up from $30.21 billion in September; the services surplus was $19.79 billion.
  • From April to October 2026-27, total exports reached $568.84 billion, up 16.66%.

What did India's October 2026 trade data show?

October 2026 was India's best export month on record: goods exports hit $49.47 billion and goods plus services reached an estimated $89.99 billion. The ministry's release shows total exports up from $69.27 billion in October 2025.

The detail matters. Merchandise imports rose to $89.35 billion from $76.69 billion, a slower climb than exports. Services exports were estimated at $40.51 billion, against $35.17 billion a year earlier, while services imports were $20.72 billion, against $17.73 billion. The services numbers are estimates, because RBI's latest services data covers September; expect a revision.

Asianet Newsable, carrying ANI's report, put the improvement in proportion: the combined deficit was 10.04% of total trade in October 2026, down from 15.36% a year earlier. That is a meaningful shift in one year, even if one strong month does not make a trend.

Which sectors drove the export jump?

Petroleum products, electronics and engineering goods drove the jump, with petroleum product exports up 140.21% and electronics up 62.70%. Every one of the five largest movers grew by more than a third.

IANS reported that petroleum product exports rose to $8.89 billion from $3.70 billion, a rise that tracks higher global fuel prices as much as volume. Electronic goods exports rose to $6.64 billion from $4.08 billion. Engineering goods, the largest single category, grew 36.01% to $12.74 billion from $9.37 billion. Gems and jewellery rose 40.06% to $3.21 billion, and organic and inorganic chemicals rose 36.20% to $2.89 billion.

The labour-intensive categories also moved. The ministry data shows ready-made garments up 20.33%, marine products up 13.76% and drugs and pharmaceuticals up 10.55%. Not everything grew: cashew exports fell 53.33% and iron ore fell 51.81%, according to ChartForest's breakdown of the release.

Is the export surge only about oil prices?

No. Strip out petroleum and gems and jewellery, and exports still rose to $37.38 billion from $28.11 billion in October, a 32.98% increase by SMEStreet's calculation.

That "core" number is the better signal for most Indian manufacturers, because it excludes the two categories most driven by commodity prices. It says buyers are ordering more engineering goods, electronics, chemicals, garments and processed food, not just paying more for fuel. The month-on-month view agrees: The Hans India reported that merchandise exports rose 11.4% from $44.40 billion in September.

Destinations widened too. The ministry's top-five list for October shows export growth of 39.25% to the USA, 48.15% to China, 139.1% to Singapore, 280.72% to Malaysia and 352.07% to Spain. NiftyTrader noted that exports grew to 43 of India's top 50 destinations. Over April to October, growth to the USA was a steadier 14.48%, which is the figure to plan around.

Why is the merchandise trade deficit still so wide?

The goods deficit stayed wide at $39.88 billion because India's fuel and electronics import bills climbed almost as fast as exports. It was narrower than October 2025's $42.58 billion but far above September.

ChartForest's breakdown shows petroleum, crude and product imports at $22.82 billion, up 54.31%, and electronic goods imports at $14.32 billion, up 49.95%. India imports more than 85% of its oil needs, as The Hans India noted, so a fuel price spike hits the import side first. Electronics is the other structural gap: exports of $6.64 billion against imports of $14.32 billion shows how much assembly still depends on imported components.

Gold helped. NiftyTrader reported gold imports of $5.51 billion, down 62.57% from $14.72 billion a year earlier. Without that fall, the October deficit would have looked much worse. Month-on-month, the goods gap widened from $30.21 billion in September, which is why the headline "deficit jumps" and "deficit narrows" stories are both technically true.

How do the April–October 2026-27 numbers compare?

Exports are growing faster than last year, but so is the gap: April–October goods exports rose 21.96% to $309.06 billion, while the combined trade deficit widened to $93.35 billion from $83.25 billion.

The ministry's cumulative table puts total exports at $568.84 billion, against $487.59 billion a year earlier, and total imports at $662.19 billion. Merchandise exports rose from $253.40 billion. The merchandise trade deficit widened to $218.15 billion from $199.46 billion, partly offset by a services surplus of $124.80 billion. Services remain the cushion: India's IT, consulting and business services exports pay for a large share of its oil and electronics bill.

Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal has said India is on track for its $1 trillion export target this year. The Hans India reported his remark that exports of about $480 billion in the first six months covered 48% of that goal. October's print keeps the target in reach, but it needs several more months like it.

What should Indian exporters and manufacturers do now?

Treat October as a demand signal, not a forecast: lock in what is working, protect margins from fuel and component costs, and get order-to-cash faster before volumes rise further.

  1. Separate price from volume. Petroleum exports grew 140.21%, in large part on price. Check your own growth the same way: split revenue change into units, price and currency before you add capacity.
  2. Map your import exposure. If your bill of materials includes imported electronics, fuel-linked inputs or chemicals, list each item, its supplier country and its contract reset date. That is where the next margin hit comes from.
  3. Follow the destination shift. Growth to Spain, Malaysia and Singapore was far ahead of growth to the USA in October. If one buyer country is more than half your exports, start qualifying a second.
  4. Fix documentation lead times. More orders mean more shipping bills, certificates of origin, LUTs and e-way bills. Manual paperwork is usually the first bottleneck when volumes jump.
  5. Forecast at SKU-destination level. A national 45.06% jump says little about your product line. Build demand forecasts from your own order book, with this data as context.

What this means for Indian businesses

For an Indian manufacturer or exporter, October 2026 confirms that overseas demand is broad and growing, with 43 of the top 50 destinations buying more. It also shows that fuel and electronics imports can eat that gain quickly. The firms that benefit will be the ones that can quote, document and ship faster while watching costs line by line. Frequently asked questions

What were India's total exports in October 2026?

India's total exports of goods and services were an estimated $89.99 billion in October 2026, up 29.90% from $69.27 billion in October 2025, according to the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. Merchandise exports were $49.47 billion, the highest monthly figure on record, and services exports were an estimated $40.51 billion. The services figure will be revised when RBI publishes October data.

What was India's trade deficit in October 2026?

India's merchandise trade deficit was $39.88 billion in October 2026, narrower than $42.58 billion a year earlier but wider than $30.21 billion in September. Including services, the combined trade deficit was $20.09 billion, down from $25.15 billion in October 2025, because a services surplus of $19.79 billion offset much of the goods gap.

Why did India's merchandise exports grow 45.06% in October 2026?

Merchandise exports grew 45.06% because of broad gains across sectors. Petroleum products rose 140.21%, helped by higher fuel prices; electronic goods rose 62.70%; engineering goods rose 36.01%; and gems and jewellery rose 40.06%. Even excluding petroleum and gems and jewellery, exports grew from $28.11 billion to $37.38 billion, so the rise was not only a price effect.

How did gold imports change in October 2026?

Gold imports fell sharply. NiftyTrader reported gold imports of $5.51 billion in October 2026, down 62.57% from $14.72 billion in October 2025. Lower gold purchases helped keep the merchandise trade deficit below last year's level, even though petroleum imports rose 54.31% to $22.82 billion and electronic goods imports rose 49.95%.

Is India on track for $1 trillion in exports this year?

Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal says yes. He said exports of about $480 billion in the first six months met 48% of the $1 trillion target. With April–October total exports at an estimated $568.84 billion, up 16.66%, India needs roughly the same monthly pace as October for the rest of the year to get there.
