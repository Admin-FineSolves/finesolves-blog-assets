Auto retail sales in India fell 23.8% year on year in October 2026 to 31.72 lakh vehicles, according to FADA data reported by KNN. The drop is mostly a calendar effect: Dhanteras and Diwali moved into November, while October 2025 had both festivals plus GST rate cuts. Sales rose 25.05% over September, and EVs set a record.

For manufacturers, component suppliers, dealers and fleet operators, this month is a test of how they read data. A headline that says "down 23.8%" can trigger cuts to production schedules, raw-material orders and dealer allocations. The underlying signals in the FADA release point the other way in several segments: record October sales for commercial vehicles, three-wheelers and tractors, a record month for electric vehicles, and passenger-vehicle dealer stock that has not moved since September.

TL;DR

  • Total auto retail sales: 31.72 lakh units in October 2026, down 23.8% year on year but up 25.05% on September.
  • Two-wheelers fell 28.35% YoY to 23.21 lakh units; rural two-wheeler sales fell 33.97% against 20.77% in cities.
  • Passenger vehicles fell 14.23% YoY to 5.08 lakh units, the third-best October on record.
  • EVs hit a record of about 3.55 lakh units, up 44.88%, taking penetration to around 11.5% from 6% a year earlier.
  • Heavy commercial vehicles grew 28.95% and construction equipment 22.07%, pointing to steady freight and infrastructure demand.

Why did auto retail sales fall 23.8% in October 2026?

Auto retail sales fell mainly because the festive calendar moved: October 2025 had Navratri, Diwali and fresh GST cuts, while this year Dhanteras and Diwali fell in November.

KNN reports that the GST 2.0 rate cuts were announced on September 22, 2025, which pushed demand into the October 2025 base. This year, Navratri ran from 11 to 20 October, according to FADA dates published by Truck Junction, but the biggest buying days, Dhanteras and Diwali, landed in November. FADA President Sai Giridhar said the yearly decline should be read against that unusually strong base, and that the 25.05% rise over September is the better festive indicator.

The month was still the second-highest October on record, DD India reported. That is not what a demand collapse looks like.

Is this a demand slowdown or a timing effect?

It is mostly a timing effect, but rural two-wheeler weakness and higher borrowing costs suggest part of the slowdown is real.

The rural numbers deserve attention. According to DD India, rural two-wheeler sales fell 33.97% year on year against 20.77% in cities. KNN reports that dealers linked part of this to Diwali shifting, but also cited localised rural stress from deficient rainfall and a weak kharif season. A calendar shift does not explain a 13-point gap between rural and urban demand.

Credit is the second watch point. Autocar Professional reports that about 31% of dealers expect a moderate or material impact on EMIs after the first repo-rate increase in over three years. Dealers remain cautiously positive: 51.65% expect November retail to grow, 34.71% expect it to stay flat and 13.64% expect a decline, according to FADA's member survey reported by DD India.

Why are commercial vehicles and construction equipment growing?

Commercial vehicles and construction equipment grew because their demand follows freight and project work, not festival dates, and both remained firm in October.

KNN reports heavy commercial vehicle retail up 28.95% and medium commercial vehicles up 21.16%, while light commercial vehicles fell 9.57%. Construction equipment grew 22.07% to 7,434 units. Commercial vehicles, three-wheelers and tractors each posted their highest-ever October, according to DD India. During the Navratri window alone, commercial vehicle retail rose 1.12% even as three-wheelers fell 7.51%, Truck Junction reported from FADA data.

For logistics and supply chain leaders, heavy-truck demand is a forward signal. Fleet owners buy heavy trucks when they expect loads. The split between strong heavy and weak light commercial vehicles suggests long-haul and infrastructure freight are firmer than last-mile and small-business transport.

How fast are EV sales in India growing?

EV sales in India hit a record of about 3.55 lakh units in October 2026, up 44.88%, with penetration near 11.5%.

According to KNN, EV penetration was around 6% a year earlier, so the share nearly doubled in twelve months. Electric two-wheelers, three-wheelers, passenger vehicles and commercial vehicles each set October records. Three-wheelers are already mostly electric: KNN puts 3W EV penetration at nearly 63%. In the Navratri window, overall EV retail grew 26.78% and electric commercial vehicles grew 96.04%, Truck Junction reported.

The passenger-vehicle fuel mix moved just as fast. Autocar Professional reports petrol's share fell from 48.96% to 41.6%, while EVs rose from 3.55% to 7.30% and hybrids from 6.96% to 9.94%. Alternative fuels together took 41.83% of passenger-vehicle sales, slightly above petrol, according to KNN.

What does dealer inventory tell manufacturers?

Dealer inventory tells manufacturers that passenger-vehicle stock is still high: 43–45 days, unchanged from September, despite strong wholesale dispatches.

DD India reports that FADA expects the Dhanteras-to-Diwali wave to bring passenger-vehicle stock down to around 30 days by the end of the calendar year. Until that happens, dealers carry the working-capital cost. The market itself is concentrated. Autocar Professional reports Maruti Suzuki held 41.75% of passenger-vehicle retail, Tata Motors 14.47%, Mahindra & Mahindra 12.80% and Hyundai 11.44%.

The year-to-date picture is healthier than the monthly one. Autocar Professional reports passenger-vehicle retail for FY2027 through October up 16.41%, while retail in the Navratri window fell 18.20% against last year's GST-boosted base.

How should manufacturers and suppliers read a calendar-shifted month?

Manufacturers and suppliers should compare festival-to-festival windows, not calendar months, and plan on sequential and year-to-date trends.

  1. Align the calendar. Compare the 2026 Navratri-to-Diwali window with the 2025 window, not October with October. Tag every week in your sales history with its festival and policy events.
  2. Weight sequential growth. A 25.05% month-on-month jump says more about current demand than a year-on-year figure distorted by a moved festival.
  3. Split rural from urban. Rural two-wheeler demand fell 33.97%. If your network is rural-heavy, plan for a slower recovery than the national figure suggests.
  4. Watch channel stock weekly. Passenger-vehicle stock at 43–45 days ties up dealer cash. Production plans should respond to retail and stock, not only to wholesale dispatches.
  5. Re-plan EV and CNG supply. With EVs at 7.30% and CNG at 24.59% of passenger-vehicle sales, parts, battery and kit supply need their own forecasts.
  6. Feed events into forecasting models. Demand-sensing and forecasting models that include festival dates, GST changes and rainfall data handle a month like this far better than ones that only see last year's sales.

What this means for Indian businesses

For auto OEMs, Tier-1 and Tier-2 suppliers, dealer groups and logistics firms, October is a reminder that a single headline number is a poor basis for a production or procurement decision. The useful signals in this release are rural weakness, firm freight demand, fast-rising EV and CNG share, and stubborn passenger-vehicle inventory. Each needs its own data feed and its own planning rule.

Frequently asked questions

How many vehicles were sold in India in October 2026?

Dealers retailed 31.72 lakh vehicles in October 2026, according to FADA data reported by KNN and DD India. That was 23.8% lower than October 2025 but 25.05% higher than September 2026. It was still the second-highest October on record, with commercial vehicles, three-wheelers and tractors each posting their best October ever.

Why are FADA October 2026 sales lower than last year?

The main reason is the festive calendar. October 2025 included both Navratri and Diwali, along with demand from GST rate cuts announced in September 2025. In 2026, Dhanteras and Diwali moved into November, so a large part of festive buying will show up in November data instead. Rural stress and higher interest rates also played a smaller role.

What is the EV share of vehicle sales in India?

EVs made up around 11.5% of all vehicle retail in October 2026, up from about 6% a year earlier, according to KNN. EV sales reached a record of about 3.55 lakh units. Within passenger vehicles, EV share was 7.30%, and within three-wheelers it was nearly 63%.

What is the dealer inventory level for passenger vehicles?

Passenger-vehicle dealer inventory stood at 43–45 days in October 2026, unchanged from September, according to FADA data reported by DD India and Autocar Professional. FADA expects stock to come down to around 30 days by the end of the calendar year as Dhanteras and Diwali sales clear vehicles from dealer yards.

What does October auto data mean for supply chain planning?

It means planners should compare festival windows rather than calendar months, track rural and urban demand separately, and plan EV and CNG supply on their own forecasts. Heavy commercial vehicle growth of 28.95% also points to firm freight demand, which matters for logistics capacity and transport costs in the coming quarter.
