Cashless claims in India are faster on paper than at the bedside. According to IRDAI data shared in the Lok Sabha, 96.69% of final discharge authorisations were cleared within the 3-hour limit, yet a LocalCircles survey found only 8% of policyholders were discharged instantly. For hospitals, the gap between insurer approval and the patient walking out is mostly a hospital-side paperwork problem that better systems can shrink.

This matters now because cashless is where the money is. IRDAI's FY25 annual report shows 66.35% of the total health claim amount was settled cashless, and the regulator has asked insurers to move towards 100% cashless processing. Every hospital that bills insurers or TPAs is being measured, by patients and by regulators, on how quickly it turns an approval into a discharge.

What are the IRDAI rules for cashless claims in 2026?

Insurers must decide a cashless pre-authorisation within 1 hour and issue final discharge authorisation within 3 hours of the hospital's request. These timelines come from IRDAI's Master Circular on Health Insurance Business, effective 31 July 2024.

ThePrint reported that the same circular requires insurers to implement ombudsman awards within 30 days, with a ₹5,000-a-day penalty for delay. In short, the regulator has put a clock on the insurer's side of the process. It has not put one on the hospital's side: preparing the discharge summary, the final bill and the document set that the TPA needs before it can approve anything.

How fast are cashless claims approved in practice?

By the insurers' own clock, fairly fast: 86.88% of pre-authorisations met the 1-hour target and 96.69% of discharge approvals met the 3-hour target between August 2024 and May 2025.

The data, shared by the Finance Ministry in the Lok Sabha and reported by Angel One, covers 63,71,620 pre-authorisation requests and 56,80,998 final authorisation cases. Only 0.77% of cases took longer than 8 hours. Those are respectable numbers for a system that handles lakhs of requests a month.

Patients tell a different story. A LocalCircles survey of 18,119 policyholders, reported by NewsMeter in March 2026, asked how long the cashless discharge took. Only 8% said it was instant, 18% said 1–3 hours and 22% said 3–6 hours. At the slow end, 15% waited 12–24 hours and 7% waited 24–48 hours.

Why is there a gap between approval time and discharge time?

Because the insurer's 3-hour clock starts only when the hospital sends a complete discharge request, and everything before that, from final billing to documents, sits on the hospital's side.

The two datasets measure different things. The government figure counts time from the hospital's request to the insurer's decision. The survey counts the patient's experience from "you can go home" to leaving the building. In between sit tasks that are usually manual in Indian hospitals: closing pharmacy and consumables entries, finalising the bill, writing and signing the discharge summary, scanning reports, and uploading all of it to a TPA portal. Any query from the TPA restarts part of the loop.

From the way we build hospital systems, this is where most delay hides: not in the insurer's decision, but in a file that is incomplete when it is first sent. A hospital cannot change the insurer's turnaround. It can control how complete and how early its request is.

How many health insurance claims are rejected in India?

About 8% of health claims were repudiated in FY25, down from 11% in FY24, according to IRDAI's annual reports.

IndiaMedToday's summary of the FY25 report shows settlements rising to 87% of registered claims from 83%, with pending claims edging down to 5% from 6%. That still means roughly 1 in 12 claimants got nothing, as NewsBytes put it. The money involved is large. Business Standard reported that health claims worth ₹26,000 crore were disallowed or repudiated in FY24, a 19.10% jump from ₹21,861 crore a year earlier. Business Today split that into ₹15,100 crore disallowed and ₹10,937 crore repudiated.

Patients see more friction than the headline ratio suggests. In the LocalCircles survey of 18,989 people who claimed between 2023 and 2025, only 32% said their claim was fully approved. Another 29% said it was partly approved for reasons they considered invalid, and 15% said it was rejected for invalid reasons.

Two shifts matter for hospital billing teams. First, the in-house share rising to 31% means more hospitals deal directly with insurer claim desks, not only TPAs, so integrations and document formats vary more. Second, the market keeps growing: NewsMeter reported that health premiums rose 9.19% to ₹1.27 lakh crore in FY2024-25. More policies means more pre-authorisations at the admission desk.

Insurer type also matters. Business Standard reported 2023-24 settlement ratios of 103.38% for public sector insurers, 88.71% for private insurers and 64.71% for standalone health insurers. A hospital whose payer mix leans to one group will see very different rejection and query patterns.

Why are health insurance complaints rising?

Complaints rose 41% in FY25 to 1,37,361, and claims issues drive most of them, according to IRDAI data reported by Angel One and NewsBytes.

NewsBytes reported that about 70% of complaints in the health and general segment related to claim rejections, delays, partial payments or documentation disputes. For hospitals, that is a reputational risk as much as a cash-flow one: the patient blames whoever is in front of them at discharge, and that is the hospital's billing desk.

How can a hospital speed up cashless claims? A 7-step checklist

  1. Start the file at admission. Capture policy, ID and pre-authorisation documents once, digitally, at the front desk, so nothing is re-collected at discharge.
  2. Track every request against the clock. Log the time each pre-authorisation and discharge request is sent and answered. Without this, nobody can tell whether delay sits with the hospital or the insurer.
  3. Close the bill continuously. Post pharmacy, consumables and procedure charges to the IPD bill in real time, not in a batch on discharge day.
  4. Use a document checklist per insurer and TPA. Most queries come from missing papers. A payer-wise checklist built into the billing system cuts round trips.
  5. Draft the discharge summary early. Let doctors start it the evening before planned discharge, so signing is the only step left.
  6. Watch enhancement requests. When a stay runs over the approved amount, send the enhancement before discharge day, not after.
  7. Review rejections monthly. Tag every partial approval and denial by reason and payer. Patterns show which documents, codes or packages need fixing.

Where AI helps today is narrow and practical: reading scanned reports into structured fields, flagging missing documents before submission, and drafting discharge summaries for a doctor to check. It does not replace the billing team's judgement on what a payer will accept.

What this means for Indian businesses

For hospital owners and administrators, cashless claims are now a throughput metric. The regulator has fixed the insurer's side at 1 hour and 3 hours. The remaining delay, which patients feel and complain about, is largely in the hospital's own workflow. The fixes are operational: a hospital management system that keeps the bill and the claim file current from admission, timestamps every request, and holds payer-specific checklists. The National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX) under ABDM aims to standardise claim exchange, which will reward hospitals whose data is already structured.

How long should cashless claim approval take in India?

Final authorisation at discharge must be issued within 3 hours of the hospital's request. Government data shows most requests met these limits between August 2024 and May 2025.

Why does cashless discharge take so long if insurers approve within 3 hours?

What is the National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX)?

NHCX is a digital gateway under the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, run by the National Health Authority, that lets hospitals, insurers and TPAs exchange health claims in a standard format. The NHA's CEO has called claim complexity one of the most persistent pain points in Indian health insurance, and a common exchange is meant to cut the manual re-entry of the same data across payers.
