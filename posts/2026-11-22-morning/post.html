The National Health Authority has proposed raising the number of PM-JAY packages reserved for government hospitals from 23 to 112, ANI reported on 18 November 2026. The 89 additions cover common conditions treated at community health centres and sub-divisional hospitals. If states agree, the change takes effect on 1 December 2026, inside a scheme that has authorised 11.69 crore admissions.

This is a small memo with a large footprint. Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY gives eligible families ₹5 lakh a year in cashless hospital cover, and private hospitals have carried most of that work: 6.74 crore of the 11.69 crore admissions authorised by 28 February 2026 were in private hospitals, according to a Lok Sabha reply published by PIB. Moving everyday treatments such as gastroenteritis, urinary tract infections and hydrocele surgery to the public side changes who gets paid for what, and it lands just as the Health Minister is pushing hospitals to digitise records.

TL;DR

  • NHA proposes reserving 112 PM-JAY packages for government hospitals, up from 23 since 2019; 89 are new.
  • States had until 25 November 2026 to comment; the proposed start date is 1 December 2026.
  • Public hospitals already make up 19,483 of 36,229 empanelled hospitals (Feb 2026), but private hospitals handled 6.74 crore of 11.69 crore admissions.
  • 2,359 hospitals have been de-empanelled and ₹328.49 crore in penalties levied for PM-JAY violations.
  • Health Minister J P Nadda said on 21 November that out-of-pocket health spending has fallen from 64% to 42%.

What did the NHA propose for PM-JAY packages?

The NHA proposed expanding the list of PM-JAY packages that only government hospitals can provide from 23 to 112, adding 89 treatments suited to community health centres and sub-divisional hospitals.

According to ANI, citing NHA sources, the authority issued an office memorandum on 13 October 2026 asking state health agencies to examine the list and send suggestions by 25 November 2026. The revised framework is proposed to apply from 1 December 2026, after state feedback. Deccan Chronicle reports that the 23 packages reserved since 2019 sit mostly in obstetrics, gynaecology and mental health.

Digital Health News lists the kind of treatments being added: severe anaemia, accelerated hypertension, acute bronchitis, diarrhoea, dysentery, gastroenteritis, urinary tract infections, renal colic, acute febrile illness, some diabetes complications, hysterectomy, circumcision, biopsy, hydrocele, haemorrhoids, cysts and lipomas. Diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma screening are also expected on the list.

Two caveats. This is a proposal, not a notified rule, and the reports say states can reduce the number of reserved packages with prior approval of their state governments. One regional report, from The News Himachal, describes the list as already directed and due in the Transaction Management System on 1 December 2026; the wire reports describe it as open for feedback. Hospitals should plan for both.

Why are PM-JAY packages being moved to government hospitals?

The stated aim is to bring routine treatment closer to rural patients and to use public facilities more, so that government hospitals earn scheme reimbursements for care they can already provide.

The capacity is there on paper. PIB data shows PM-JAY empanelment grew from 6,917 hospitals in FY 2018-19 to 36,229 by 28 February 2026. In 2018-19 private hospitals led, 3,904 to 3,013. By February 2026 public hospitals led, 19,483 to 16,746.

Usage has not followed the count. The same Lok Sabha reply shows 6.74 crore of 11.69 crore authorised admissions went to private hospitals. More public hospitals on the list does not mean more PM-JAY patients walking into them. Reserving packages is a blunt way to close that gap: if a CHC can treat gastroenteritis, the scheme stops paying a private hospital to do it.

How big is PM-JAY now?

PM-JAY has now funded more than 13 crore hospitalisations and treatment worth over ₹2 lakh crore, Health Minister J P Nadda said on 21 November 2026.

Open Magazine, reporting government figures at the scheme's eighth anniversary, put coverage at more than 60 crore people, 48.54 crore Ayushman cards and 13.25 crore admissions worth ₹2.03 lakh crore as of 31 August 2026. The benefit package spans 1,961 procedures across 27 specialities. The 112 reserved packages would still be a small slice of that catalogue, but they are high-volume, short-stay cases.

What does this mean for private hospitals?

Private hospitals in states that adopt the list would stop receiving PM-JAY payments for the 89 added treatments, so small and mid-sized hospitals that rely on scheme volumes need to check their case mix now.

The exposed hospitals are the ones whose PM-JAY revenue comes from short medical admissions and minor surgery rather than cardiac, oncology or orthopaedic work. A nursing home that admits a steady stream of gastroenteritis, fever and UTI cases under the scheme could see that volume move. Larger tertiary hospitals are less affected, because the reserved list targets care a CHC or SDH can deliver.

The scheme is also tighter on compliance than it was. ETV Bharat reports that 2,359 hospitals have been de-empanelled for violations, 29 FIRs filed and ₹328.49 crore in penalties levied. AI and ML checks flag suspicious claims within 24 hours of submission, and claims confirmed as fraudulent saved ₹676.14 crore as on 31 July 2026. A hospital that keeps billing a newly reserved package by mistake will be caught by the system, not by an auditor months later.

What does this mean for government hospitals?

Government hospitals gain reimbursable volume, but only if they can raise clean pre-authorisations and claims on time.

PIB states that PM-JAY claims are to be settled within 15 days of submission for in-state hospitals and 30 days for portability claims. Those timelines assume the hospital submits complete documentation. A CHC that has rarely billed the scheme now has to register the patient, check eligibility, raise pre-authorisation, attach discharge papers and track the claim.

This is where the money is won or lost. Digital Health News notes the change could raise hospital earnings from reimbursements, but reimbursement only arrives for claims that are raised. Records matter too: Nadda used his 21 November JIPMER convocation speech to urge young doctors to adopt e-prescriptions and digitise records under ABHA, and ETV Bharat reports 95.99 crore ABHA IDs created as on 31 July 2026.

How should hospitals prepare before 1 December?

Hospitals should map their PM-JAY case mix against the proposed list, update package masters in their billing system, and train front-desk staff before the state notifies its final list.

  1. Get the state list, not the national one. States can trim the 112, so watch your State Health Agency's notification rather than news reports.
  2. Run a case-mix check. Pull the last 12 months of PM-JAY claims and tag each against the proposed reserved list. Private hospitals see revenue at risk; public hospitals see the volume they should now be capturing.
  3. Update package masters. Your hospital management system's tariff and package tables should match the Transaction Management System on day one, so staff do not raise claims that will be rejected.
  4. Fix pre-authorisation at the desk. Many claim problems start at admission: missing documents, wrong package codes, eligibility not verified. A checklist built into the admission screen catches them early.
  5. Track claims to payment. Measure days from discharge to claim submission and from submission to settlement against the 15-day and 30-day norms.
  6. Link records to ABHA. Digitised discharge summaries and e-prescriptions make claim documentation faster and are where policy is heading.

Frequently asked questions

What are PM-JAY packages?

PM-JAY packages are the defined treatments and procedures that hospitals can provide cashless under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, each with a set rate. The health benefit package covers 1,961 procedures across 27 specialities, and eligible families get up to ₹5 lakh a year in hospital cover.

How many PM-JAY packages are reserved for government hospitals?

Since 2019, 23 packages, mostly in obstetrics, gynaecology and mental health, have been reserved for public hospitals. The National Health Authority has proposed raising this to 112 by adding 89 packages, with a proposed effective date of 1 December 2026 after states send feedback.

Can private hospitals still treat these conditions under Ayushman Bharat?

Private hospitals can still treat the patients, but in states that adopt the reserved list they would not be paid under PM-JAY for those packages. States may reduce the number of reserved packages with prior approval of their state governments, so the final list can differ by state.

How long does PM-JAY take to settle a claim?

According to a Lok Sabha reply published by PIB, claims are to be settled within 15 days of submission for hospitals in the beneficiary's own state and within 30 days for portability claims from other states. Claims sent with incomplete documentation can take longer.

How does PM-JAY detect fraudulent claims?

PM-JAY uses AI and machine-learning checks that flag suspicious claims within 24 hours of submission, followed by review. ETV Bharat reports 2,359 hospitals de-empanelled, 29 FIRs and ₹328.49 crore in penalties, with ₹676.14 crore saved from fraudulent claims as on 31 July 2026.
